# WM-v0 D1 baseline audit

## Disposition

The accepted baseline contains six real simulator futures from three exact
PickCube anchors. None of the six candidates was generated by policy inference at
its anchor. All are frozen synthetic transformations, so the real-policy count is
zero and no checkpoint identity applies. All six terminal outcomes are success.

Evidence is the compact run
`20260721T053204Z_wm-v0-smoke_990d8ce_seed271828`, its accepted dataset manifest,
provenance summary, and execution audit. The tracked result commit is
`c786e5d03b598ccd4e9f22de25486f87c6c162a4`; collection initially executed at
`990d8ce1dd9c303e3295bad41320ca1a29c72607` and the explicit backbone-loading fix
executed at `bafe920b777778519b2df2b2c0e10139e059b4e1`.

## Candidate and class diagnosis

The only candidate families are three additive Gaussian perturbations and three
constant biases. Both are direct action edits prohibited from the
`policy_generated` class. Candidate diversity is therefore two transformations,
one common late-transport phase, no policy/checkpoint diversity, and no legal
sampling/configuration diversity.

All anchors occur late in successful source plans and the two mild perturbations
remain recoverable, explaining the single success class. The audit found no real
ACT, diffusion, fallback, earlier-checkpoint, or stochastic policy provider that
could legally supply varied actions. The server also lacks the accepted LangMani
registry, selection record, checkpoint, and processor artifacts required to
reconstruct such a provider.

## Throughput and utilization

Collection attempt 2 ran from `05:34:40Z` to `05:35:19Z`: 39 seconds for three
anchors and six candidates. The observed averages are 13.0 seconds per anchor and
6.5 seconds per candidate; a linear 100-sample estimate is about 650 seconds
(10.8 minutes), excluding startup, publication, and contention.

GPU memory was 0 MiB before and after the recorded stages. One live collection
observation saw about 1.9 GiB allocated and roughly 49% GPU utilization; this was
not continuous telemetry. CPU utilization was not recorded and is therefore
unknown. Feature construction took about 2 seconds, smoke training 17 seconds,
and evaluation 6 seconds, but those timings are not data-collection throughput.

Candidate inference can be batched per anchor if a real provider supports it.
Each simulator candidate must still use an independently restored session, and
steps within one trajectory are sequential. Different anchors could run in
isolated workers only after a separate resource/determinism probe; D1 initially
uses one worker to preserve the accepted simulator contract.

## Integrity, retry, and label audit

The first collection attempt failed on a source/split-group identity mismatch and
wrote zero samples. The second succeeded. There was no silent retry, but the old
CLI buffered all anchors in memory and wrote only after the whole collection, so
it lacked per-anchor durability, duplicate-content rejection, and safe resume.
D1 replaces this with staging, completion markers, quarantine of incomplete
anchors, atomic manifest/index/state updates, and zero-work complete resume.

Future progress was observed at every one of 24 future boundaries and is not a
copied terminal label. `grasped`, `dropped`, `object_displacement`, and
`task_success` were observed at all 24 boundaries. Collision, timeout, workspace,
and wrong-contact channels were unavailable and explicitly masked, yielding a
50% aggregate event missing ratio. The data remains physically real but too small,
single-class, and entirely synthetic-candidate for formal training.
